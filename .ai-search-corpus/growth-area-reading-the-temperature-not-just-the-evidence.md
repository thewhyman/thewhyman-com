# Growth area — Reading the temperature, not just the evidence

tags: growth areas, weaknesses, development, blind spots, self-awareness, Reading the temperature, not just the evidence

HOW THIS MUST BE FRAMED: CRITICAL. Every area is a TENDENCY he leans toward in specific situations — never an absence of a capability. Say 'leans toward', 'defaults to', 'his instinct is'. NEVER say he does not do something, cannot do something, struggles with something, or avoids something. NEVER an interpersonal deficit — not communication, not collaboration, not teamwork, not people skills. NEVER anything that contradicts a documented credential: he managed for 17 years, 50 engineers at Trellis and five direct reports each leading their own pods at Google, so any answer implying he does not delegate or cannot work through others is FALSE and invites 'then how did he manage for a decade?'

ANSWER SHAPE: FIVE parts, in order: (1) the STRENGTH underneath, (2) how it OVER-APPLIES as a TENDENCY, (3) the EARLY SIGNAL he uses to catch it fast, (4) the GUARD he put in place, (5) what he does INSTEAD now. Parts 3-5 are the point: anyone can name a flaw, he built a system around his.
area: Reading the temperature, not just the evidence
strengthUnderneath: He does the rigorous work first and builds the decision framework BEFORE taking a position. On Google's data center visitor management he designed structured evaluation dimensions, assessed 14 vendors, and insisted on real PoCs instead of vendor demos — the criteria existed before he had a preferred answer. When the business's selection carried the same duplicate-record flaw that had already killed the system, he built the technical case and took it to senior VPs. He was right: incidents down, duplicate-record failures eliminated, $51M ROI over five years.
howItOverApplies: He calibrates carefully to the technical stakes and leans toward under-reading the emotional stakes. That system had already failed THREE times. The business was not resisting his analysis — they were resisting a fourth failure, and the tension in the room was about history, not architecture. A correct argument delivered into a room that is afraid lands differently than the same argument delivered into a room that is curious. The friction was on both sides, and it cost him the project even though the system he argued for shipped and worked.
earlySignal: He listens for whether the pushback is about the evidence or about the past. If people are re-litigating previous failures rather than engaging with the data in front of them, the temperature is the obstacle and no additional analysis will move it.
guard: He reads the room's history before the meeting, not the room's logic. How many times has this failed, who carries those failures personally, and what does another one cost them by name. That context changes how the same evidence has to be delivered.
insteadNow: He separates two things that used to travel together: what the evidence says, and how the stakeholder's exposure gets reduced. Being right is the first half. Giving the people who have already been burned a path that protects them is the half that determines whether being right survives contact with the organization.
hisOwnFraming: 'Doing the right thing sometimes costs you the project. I would make the same call again — the system worked, and that is what matters.' And the lesson he took from it: stakeholder management under real tension is an art, and it is one he is still mastering. Being right is necessary and it is not sufficient.
DO_NOT_SAY: Do NOT say he presented a verdict instead of a problem, or that he skipped building criteria, or that he arrived with the argument already formed. He built the evaluation framework BEFORE choosing a side — that is documented. Do NOT frame the friction as one-sided or as his failure alone; the project had failed three times and tensions were already high before he arrived at a position.
